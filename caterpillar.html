Case study 05 / Bucknell University · March 2023–May 2024

Caterpillar-Inspired Soft Robot

Exploring how tendon-driven compliant segments can produce crawling, climbing, and hanging motion.

Engineering focus

Compliant mechanisms · Tendon actuation · Gait design · Force measurement

Tools

SolidWorks · PLA / Filaflex · MATLAB · Arduino / C++

Challenge

The project investigated locomotion in a caterpillar-inspired soft robot. Compliant segments, tendon forces, and actuator timing needed to work together to produce coordinated motion.

My role

Working with Prof. William Scott at Bucknell, I designed flexible bending segments and mechanisms, fabricated prototypes, collected force and motion data, and developed motor-control and gait sequences.

Mechanical design and fabrication

I used SolidWorks to design tendon-driven flexible segments and bistable mechanisms. Rigid PLA and flexible Filaflex parts allowed me to build and iterate the structure while exploring how geometry and compliance affected bending and motion.

Measurement and control

I connected force sensors to Arduino hardware and used MATLAB with camera-based tracking to analyze motion. Mechanical models of tendon force transmission and segment deformation supported interpretation of the experiments.

I implemented Arduino/C++ and MATLAB-based closed-loop motor control to coordinate multiple actuators, and developed gait patterns for crawling, climbing, and hanging experiments.

Outcome

The work produced fabricated mechanisms, experimental force and motion measurements, and physically tested gait sequences. I presented the research at Bucknell student research events and the Kalman Research Symposium.
